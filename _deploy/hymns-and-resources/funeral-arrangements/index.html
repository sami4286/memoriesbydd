Practical guidance

What to do after someone dies.

A calm starting checklist for England and Wales. Rules differ in Scotland and Northern Ireland, so use the official links for the place where the person died.

Start here

You do not need to solve every decision today.

Deal first with the legal registration and the people who can help. Wording, photographs and booklet choices can follow in stages.

The first practical steps

1. Wait for medical confirmation

A doctor and medical examiner confirm the cause of death before the register office can complete the registration. If a coroner is involved, the procedure and timing can differ.

2. Register the death

GOV.UK says to register within five days of confirmation in England and Wales, or eight days in Scotland. The registrar explains the documents needed for burial or cremation.

3. Use Tell Us Once

The registrar can provide the reference for Tell Us Once, which reports the death to most government organisations in one process. Banks, utilities, insurers and some private pensions still need separate contact.

4. Choose the funeral arrangements

A funeral director can coordinate the person’s care, venue, burial or cremation, transport and timings. Ask for an itemised estimate and clarify which third-party costs are included.

5. Shape the service

Confirm the date, officiant or celebrant, music, readings, speakers and running order. The funeral booklet can begin with incomplete information and be refined before approval.

6. Check current financial support

Eligibility and payment amounts change. Use the current GOV.UK guidance instead of relying on figures copied from an older guide.
