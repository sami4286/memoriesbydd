Resources — Practical Help

Bereavement Support

Money is the last thing anyone wants to think about, and it arrives at the worst possible time. This is a plain-English summary of what help exists in the UK.

A guide, not advice — and deliberately without figures

Benefit rates change every April. Rather than print an amount that will quietly go out of date, every section below links to the GOV.UK page that holds the current figure. Please check there before you rely on anything.

If you are struggling, Citizens Advice will help you in person and it is free.

Bereavement Support Payment

Help if your husband, wife or civil partner has died. It is normally paid as a first larger sum followed by monthly payments, and it does not affect most other benefits for the first year. There are time limits on claiming, so it is worth checking early even if you are not sure you qualify.

Current rates and how to claim on GOV.UK

Funeral Expenses Payment

Help with funeral costs if you are receiving certain benefits and are responsible for arranging the funeral. It can go towards burial or cremation fees, a contribution to other costs, and travel. It does not usually cover a funeral in full.

Who can claim, and how, on GOV.UK

If you are in Scotland

Funeral support is devolved and works differently. It is called Funeral Support Payment and is administered by Social Security Scotland.

Funeral Support Payment on mygov.scot

Tell Us Once

A free service that reports a death to most government departments in one go — HMRC, DWP, the passport office, the DVLA and usually the local council. The registrar will give you a reference number when you register the death. It saves a great many phone calls at a time when you should not have to make them.

How Tell Us Once works on GOV.UK

What to do in the first week

There is a legal order to some of it and GOV.UK sets it out step by step — getting the medical certificate, registering the death, and what a registrar will ask you for.

What to do after someone dies, on GOV.UK

Where to get help from a person

  • Citizens Advice — free, in person or by phone, on benefits and on money generally.
  • Cruse Bereavement Support — a free helpline and one-to-one support for grief itself.
  • Your funeral director. They deal with these forms every week and will usually help you with them without being asked.
